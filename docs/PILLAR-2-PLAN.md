# Pillar 2 — Project Plan

**Working title:** "Music in Special Education — A Practical Guide for Teachers, Therapists, and Administrators"
**URL slug:** `/education/guide-music-in-special-education/`
**Word target:** 5,000-5,500 (≤6,000 hard cap — denser than Pillar 1)
**Why this pillar:** the SPED + music-therapy niche is institutional-buyer gold. Music therapists are *very* loyal once you earn their trust, and almost no commercial brand has written the definitive guide. Lincoln Center, Stanford d.school, and Trossingen all have SPED-adjacent angles we can credibly cite. Our Lesson 5 ("Composing for the Body") is already accessibility-first — the pillar is the long-form pedagogical home for that lesson.

**Risk:** SPED writing requires extreme care. Person-first language. Non-exploitative framing. No "inspiration porn." If we get the tone wrong, we lose the trust we're trying to build *and* invite reputational damage. The plan accounts for this with a tone-and-language section + an external reviewer step before publish.

### Research papers / books to cite
- Loewy, J. (multiple) — music therapy in NICU + hospital contexts
- Bandura, A. — self-efficacy theory (general but applies to "I made music for the first time")
- Welch, G. — UK-side music + learning research
- IDEA (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act) text — for IEP section
- Recent (2023+) systematic reviews on music intervention in autism spectrum (be precise: distinguish music-ed from music-therapy claims)

### Cite-style
- Use links inline. Footnotes feel academic, alienating for the audience.
- Quote sparingly, never more than 15 words at a time (copyright + AI-detection sensitivity).

## 4. Sensitivity + tone rules (binding)

These are not optional. Get these wrong and the pillar becomes a liability.

1. **Person-first language by default.** "Student with autism" not "autistic student" — though identity-first is also valid for adult self-advocates. Use the term the disability community is currently using. Verify with current AAC self-advocacy guidance (Autism Self-Advocacy Network — ASAN — is the standard).
2. **No "inspiration porn."** Stories of disabled students making music are not "moving" or "heartwarming" because the student is disabled. They are normal student achievements. Treat them as such.
3. **No medical claims.** We are music-ed, not music therapy. Do NOT claim music technology "treats" anything. Music *educates*. Music *engages*. Music can be a *vehicle* for therapy — but the therapy claim requires a board-certified music therapist.
4. **No before/after framing that defines students by their deficit.** A non-verbal student who plays a note has done something musically real. Not "overcome their disability" — they did music.
5. **Cite real practitioners by name when possible.** Music therapists with research records. SPED music teachers with publications. Establishes the guide's authority is borrowed, not claimed.
6. **Acknowledge what we don't know.** "Research on music for non-speaking students is sparse and methodologically limited. The strongest evidence is X; the weakest is Y."
7. **The Playtronica disclosure stays prominent.** Last section, exactly as Pillar 1.

## 6. Schema additions

Beyond the inherited Article + Course-page schema:

- **`HowTo` schema** on §6 (the three concrete lessons) — Google's HowTo schema rewards step-rich content. The three lesson templates can each emit a sub-`HowTo` block.
- **`MedicalGuideline` schema** — NO. Tempting because of the SPED context, but `MedicalGuideline` triggers medical content guidelines from Google. We don't want to be flagged as medical. Avoid.
- **`Article` + `EducationalAudience`** — set audience type to "Educator" + "SpecialEducation". Google understands this taxonomy.
- **`Person` schema** for any quoted practitioners (with their permission + verification). Establishes credibility.

## 8. Writing process + sequence

The pillar should be written in this order, **not** front to back:

1. **First — write §5 (Equipment by need-profile).** This is the section with the highest commercial intent and the most concrete material. Writing it first surfaces the framing decisions early.
2. **Then — write §6 (Three lesson templates).** Concrete material drives the abstract framing in §1-3.
3. **Then — write §1-3 (Why / Research / Roles).** With concrete material in hand, the abstract intro writes itself.
4. **Then — write §4 (Template) + §7 (IEP) + §11 (Mistakes).** Practical interstitials.
5. **Then — write §8 (Music ed vs music therapy) + §9 (Sensory rooms) + §10 (Procurement).** Specialized sections.
6. **Then — TL;DR + §12-14 (Closing).** Frame what's been written.

**Time estimate:** 14-18 hours of focused writing time, distributed over 2-3 weeks. Not 14 hours in one go — pillar-writing fatigue produces shallow content.

## 16. What we're NOT doing

- We are NOT writing a music therapy textbook. Music therapy requires MT-BC training; we are music-ed.
- We are NOT replacing existing SPED music resources (NAfME's, Drake Music's, AMTA's). We complement them with a vendor-neutral practical guide.
- We are NOT making any therapeutic claim. Music technology *engages* students; it doesn't *treat* anything.
- We are NOT writing in academic style. Plain language. Real classrooms. Specific examples.
- We are NOT outsourcing to AI. Pillar 2's voice and authority depend on Andrey's perspective (or a credited human collaborator). AI assists with structure, never with original argumentation.
